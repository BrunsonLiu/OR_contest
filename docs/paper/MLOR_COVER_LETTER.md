**Re: Submission of “Inventory age and store minimum remaining shelf life in warehouse-to-store distribution”**

Dear Editor,

We submit the enclosed manuscript for consideration as a research article in *Computers & Industrial Engineering*.

The paper studies a warehouse-to-store distribution problem in which the inventory at a warehouse is not homogeneous: batches differ in age, and stores accept a delivery only if its remaining shelf life at arrival is at least a store-specific minimum (MLOR). Because travel time consumes shelf life, an old batch has a shorter effective service radius. The planner must decide which warehouses to open, which store is assigned to which warehouse, how vehicles are routed, and which inventory age serves each store.

We formulate the problem as a mixed-integer program and show that the shelf-life condition reduces to an arrival deadline determined jointly by warehouse age and store threshold. The route-feasibility subproblem is NP-complete even for one warehouse and equal thresholds; a descending-MLOR order is not generally feasible when some order is. We solve 8- and 12-store instances exactly by subset dynamic programming and develop an age-feasible adaptive large neighbourhood search (ALNS) for larger instances.

The computational study is designed around the claims rather than around a single performance table. Against an age-blind exact plan, the age-aware plan is better in 9/10 eight-store and 7/10 twelve-store instances and never worse. On held-out twelve-store instances, the tuned ALNS reaches the exact optimum in 9/10 cases (mean gap 1.78), and the improvement is reported with confidence intervals. The ALNS is compared with three alternative metaheuristics under the same number of solution evaluations (simulated annealing, tabu search and a genetic algorithm), with a freshest-first rule, with a rule-plus-local-search baseline, and with an LP lower bound at 20 stores. A 2×2 check shows that the age–distance–threshold interaction, rather than the cold-chain label, drives the difference.

The contribution is deliberately positioned as an application/extension. The MLOR concept has been studied in an operations–marketing setting, and perishable inventory-routing models already track remaining shelf life; the difference studied here is the joint treatment of warehouse age, a store-specific delivery threshold and location-routing decisions. We state the scope explicitly: the model is single-period, deterministic, single-echelon and unit-demand, and all parameters are synthetic. The paper does not claim field savings or a confirmed new problem class.

We believe the work fits the applied optimization and industrial decision-making scope of *Computers & Industrial Engineering*: it formulates an operational problem, connects its structure to the solution method, and reports exact, heuristic and statistical evidence with explicit limitations.

The manuscript is original, has not been published elsewhere, and is not under consideration by another journal. All authors have approved the submission. The synthetic instances are generated by the code described in the paper; the real-network illustration uses the contest location, warehouse and order files. No confidential data were used.
